Title: Anubis, Guardian of the Dead and Embalming
Category: Egyptian Deities & Legends
Keywords: Anubis, Inpu, underworld, embalming, mummification, Duat, Weighing of the Heart, Maat, jackal

In ancient Egyptian religion, Anubis (known to Egyptians as Inpu or Anpu) is the jackal-headed god of death, mummification, embalming, the afterlife, lost souls, and the underworld. He is one of the oldest deities of ancient Egypt, preceding Osiris as lord of the dead during the Old Kingdom.

Iconography and Appearance:
Anubis was portrayed either as an anthropomorphic figure with the body of a man and the black head of a jackal or desert dog, or entirely as a reclining black canine. The color black was symbolic of rebirth, the rich fertile soil of the Nile river valley, and the discoloration of the corpse during mummification.

Roles in the Egyptian Afterlife:
- Lord of the Sacred Land: Master of cemeteries and necropolises, protecting graves against tomb robbers and wild scavengers.
- Patron of Embalming: Credited with inventing the sacred rites of mummification when he helped Isis preserve the broken body of Osiris.
- Guide of Souls (Psychopomp): Safely led deceased souls through the perilous challenges of the underworld (Duat) to the Hall of Two Truths.
- The Weighing of the Heart (Psychostasia): Anubis performed the most solemn judgment of Egyptian theology. He placed the deceased's heart (ib) on the Great Scales against the Feather of Ma'at (representing cosmic truth, order, and morality). If the heart was balanced or lighter than the feather, the soul proceeded to the paradise of Aaru. If heavy with sin, the demoness Ammit (devourer of souls) consumed the heart, causing eternal oblivion.
