Title: Odin, Allfather of Norse Mythology
Category: Norse Deities & Legends
Keywords: Odin, Allfather, Wotan, Yggdrasil, runes, Huginn, Muninn, Sleipnir, Gungnir, Valhalla, wisdom, war

In Norse mythology, Odin (from Old Norse Odinn) is the supreme god of the Aesir pantheon, associated with wisdom, healing, death, royalty, the gallows, war, victory, sorcery, poetry, frenzy, and the runic alphabet. He is often called the Allfather (Alfodr) as the progenitor of many gods.

The Quest for Wisdom:
Odin is defined by an insatiable, obsessive quest for occult knowledge:
- Sacrifice of His Eye: Odin traveled to the spring of Mimir beneath the roots of the world tree Yggdrasil to drink from the waters of cosmic knowledge. Mimir required Odin to pluck out one of his eyes and leave it in the well as payment.
- The Hanging on Yggdrasil: To learn the secret wisdom of the magical runes, Odin hung himself from the wind-swept tree of Yggdrasil for nine nights, wounded by his own spear, consecrated to himself. At the brink of death, the runes revealed themselves to him.

Companions and Relics:
- Gungnir: A spear forged by the dwarves that never misses its mark.
- Draupnir: A magical gold ring that produces eight matching gold rings every ninth night.
- Huginn and Muninn: Two ravens ("Thought" and "Memory") that fly across the entire world daily and whisper secrets into Odin's ears.
- Geri and Freki: Two wolves that accompany him at his feast table.
- Sleipnir: An eight-legged steed, offspring of Loki, capable of traversing sky and underworld.
- Valhalla: Odin's magnificent hall in Asgard, where the Valkyries bring the Einherjar (fallen warriors) to train for the prophesied war of Ragnarok, where Odin is fated to be devoured by the monstrous wolf Fenrir.
