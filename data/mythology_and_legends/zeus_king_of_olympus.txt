Title: Zeus, King of the Olympian Gods
Category: Greek Deities & Legends
Keywords: Zeus, Olympus, thunderbolt, Kronos, Rhea, Hera, lightning, justice, eagle, Aegis

In ancient Greek religion and mythology, Zeus is the king of the gods, ruler of Mount Olympus, and god of the sky, weather, thunder, lightning, law, order, and justice. He is the youngest child of the Titans Kronos and Rhea. Kronos had swallowed all his older siblings (Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, and Poseidon) out of fear of being overthrown. Rhea hid baby Zeus in a cave on Mount Ida in Crete, tricking Kronos into swallowing a swaddled stone.

Overthrow of the Titans:
Upon reaching adulthood, Zeus forced Kronos to disgorge his siblings. Joined by the hundred-handed Hecatoncheires and the Cyclopes whom Zeus freed from Tartarus, Zeus led the Olympian gods in a ten-year war known as the Titanomachy. The Cyclopes forged Zeus's signature weapon: the devastating thunderbolt. After defeating the Titans, Zeus cast them into Tartarus and drew lots with his brothers: Zeus won dominion over the sky, Poseidon the seas, and Hades the underworld.

Symbols and Powers:
- Thunderbolt: Capable of sundering mountains and smiting divine and mortal foes.
- Aegis: An awe-inspiring protective cloak or shield decorated with the head of the Gorgon, producing terror in enemies.
- Sacred Animals and Plants: The golden eagle (symbolizing supremacy) and the oak tree.
- Consort and Children: Married to Hera, queen of the heavens, Zeus also fathered many famous deities and heroes, including Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Hermes, Perseus, and Heracles.
