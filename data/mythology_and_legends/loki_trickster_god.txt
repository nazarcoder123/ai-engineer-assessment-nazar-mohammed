Title: Loki, the Trickster God of Norse Myth
Category: Norse Deities & Legends
Keywords: Loki, trickster, shapeshifter, Asgard, Fenrir, Jormungandr, Hel, Baldr, Ragnarok

In Norse mythology, Loki is a cunning trickster god, shapeshifter, and catalyst for both humor and cosmic tragedy. The son of the giant Farbauti and Laufey, Loki is not by blood an Aesir god, but became blood-brother to Odin through an ancient sacred oath.

Shapeshifting and Offspring:
Loki possessed the power to change his sex and form into a fly, a salmon, a mare, or a seal. With the giantess Angrboda, Loki fathered three monstrous entities central to Norse eschatology:
1. Fenrir: The ferocious giant wolf destined to kill Odin at Ragnarok.
2. Jormungandr: The Midgard Serpent that wraps around the human realm.
3. Hel: The half-living, half-corpse goddess who rules the underworld realm of the dead.
By turning into a mare, Loki also gave birth to Sleipnir, Odin's eight-legged steed.

The Death of Baldr:
Loki's greatest crime was engineering the murder of Baldr, the most beloved and pure of the gods. Knowing that all living things except mistletoe had sworn never to harm Baldr, Loki guided the blind god Hodr to throw a dart made of mistletoe, which pierced Baldr's heart.
As punishment, the gods bound Loki to stones using the entrails of his own son, with a venomous snake dripping corrosive venom onto his face until the arrival of Ragnarok, when Loki will break free to captain the ship of the dead against the Aesir.
