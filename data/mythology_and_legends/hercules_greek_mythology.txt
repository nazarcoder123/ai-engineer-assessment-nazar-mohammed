Title: Heracles (Hercules) in Greek Mythology
Category: Greek Heroes & Demigods
Keywords: Heracles, Hercules, Zeus, Alcmene, Hera, Twelve Labours, Nemean Lion, Hydra, Cerberus, Olympus

In Greek mythology, Heracles (later Romanized as Hercules) is the greatest of all Greek heroes, celebrated for his superhuman physical strength, courage, ingenuity, and perseverance. He was the son of Zeus (king of the gods) and the mortal woman Alcmene. Zeus's wife Hera despised Heracles from birth, sending two deadly serpents to his crib, which the infant Heracles throttled with his bare hands.

The Twelve Labours of Heracles:
Driven into temporary madness by Hera, Heracles tragically slew his children. To expiate his crime, the Delphic Oracle commanded him to serve King Eurystheus of Tiryns, who assigned him twelve seemingly impossible tasks:
1. Slay the Nemean Lion: Whose golden fur was impenetrable by mortal weapons; Heracles choked it to death and wore its pelt as armor.
2. Slay the nine-headed Lernaean Hydra: Whose heads multiplied when severed; Heracles and his nephew Iolaus cauterized the stumps with firebrands.
3. Capture the Golden Hind of Ceryneia: Sacred to Artemis, exceptionally swift, captured alive after a year-long pursuit.
4. Capture the Erymanthian Boar: Trapped in deep snowdrifts on Mount Erymanthos.
5. Clean the Augean Stables in a single day: Heracles diverted the rivers Alpheus and Peneus to wash out the filthy stables.
6. Defeat the Stymphalian Birds: Man-eating birds with bronze beaks; scared into flight with bronze clappers forged by Hephaestus and shot down.
7. Capture the Cretan Bull: A savage bull ravaging Crete; wrestled down and brought back alive.
8. Steal the Mares of Diomedes: Flesh-eating horses owned by the Thracian giant Diomedes.
9. Obtain the Girdle of Hippolyta: The belt of the fierce Amazon queen.
10. Steal the Cattle of the three-bodied giant Geryon.
11. Steal the golden apples of the Hesperides: Aided by the Titan Atlas, while Heracles temporarily shouldered the celestial heavens.
12. Capture and bring back Cerberus: The three-headed hound guarding the gates of Hades, subdued with bare hands without using weapons.

Death and Apotheosis:
Later in life, poisoned by the venom of the Hydra soaked into the shirt of Nessus, Heracles constructed his own funeral pyre. Zeus ordered the clouds to receive him, and Heracles was granted immortality (apotheosis), ascending to Mount Olympus where he married Hebe, the goddess of youth.
