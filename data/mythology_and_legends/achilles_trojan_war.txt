Title: Achilles and the Trojan War
Category: Greek Heroes & Legends
Keywords: Achilles, Iliad, Trojan War, Styx, Thetis, Patroclus, Hector, invulnerability, heel

In Greek legend, Achilles is the preeminent hero of Homer's Iliad and the greatest warrior among the Achaean (Greek) coalition that besieged Troy. He was the son of Peleus, mortal king of Phthia, and Thetis, an immortal sea nymph (Nereid).

Invulnerability and the River Styx:
According to classical traditions popularized by Statius, his mother Thetis sought to render Achilles immortal by dipping him into the River Styx. The waters made his entire body impervious to harm, except for the heel by which she held him—the origin of the proverbial "Achilles' heel" denoting a fatal point of vulnerability.

Feats in the Trojan War:
Achilles led the Myrmidons, elite fierce warriors renowned for discipline. When the Greek supreme commander Agamemnon insulted Achilles by confiscating the captive Briseis, Achilles withdrew from combat in rage, causing devastating Greek losses.
Achilles returned to the fray with apocalyptic fury only after his beloved companion Patroclus was slain by Hector, the Trojan prince. Achilles chased Hector thrice around the walls of Troy, killed him in single combat, and dragged Hector's corpse behind his war chariot.
Achilles was eventually killed near the Scaean Gates of Troy when the Trojan prince Paris shot a poisoned arrow that struck his vulnerable heel, an arrow guided by the archer god Apollo.
