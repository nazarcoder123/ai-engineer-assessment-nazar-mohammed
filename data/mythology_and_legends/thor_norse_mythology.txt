Title: Thor in Norse Mythology
Category: Norse Deities & Legends
Keywords: Thor, Donar, Mjolnir, thunder, lightning, Odin, Asgard, Midgard, Jormungandr, Megingjord

In Norse mythology, Thor (Old Norse: Thorr) is the hammer-wielding god associated with thunder, lightning, storms, sacred groves and trees, strength, the protection of humankind, and also hallowing and fertility. Thor is the son of Odin (the Allfather) and the earth goddess Jord (Fyorgyn). He is married to the golden-haired goddess Sif.

Attributes and Possessions:
- Mjolnir: Thor's mighty hammer, forged by the dwarven brothers Sindri and Brokkr. It never misses its target, crushes anything it strikes, and returns automatically to Thor's hand when thrown.
- Megingjord: A magical belt of power that doubles Thor's already formidable divine physical strength when fastened.
- Jarnreipr: A pair of iron gauntlets that Thor requires to properly grip and wield Mjolnir.
- Chariot of Goats: Thor travels in a chariot pulled by two magical goats, Tanngrisnir (Teeth-barer) and Tanngnjostr (Teeth-grinder), which can be killed and eaten each night and resurrect the next morning if their bones remain unbroken.

Mythological Feats and Lore:
Thor resides in Thrudheim ("Place of Might") in his great hall Bilskirnir, which contains 540 rooms. Unlike the blonde depiction popularized by modern comic books, classical Norse texts describe Thor as having fierce red hair, a full red beard, and flashing red eyes that burn with lightning.

Thor's ultimate nemesis is Jormungandr (the Midgard Serpent), the monstrous child of Loki that encircles the entire world of Midgard. During Ragnarok (the twilight of the gods), Thor is prophesied to battle Jormungandr, slay the serpent with a devastating strike from Mjolnir, but take only nine steps before collapsing dead from the beast's deadly venom.
