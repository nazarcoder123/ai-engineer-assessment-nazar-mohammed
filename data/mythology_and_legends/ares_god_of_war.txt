Title: Ares, Greek God of War and Bloodshed
Category: Greek Deities & Legends
Keywords: Ares, Mars, war, violence, bloodshed, Olympus, Phobos, Deimos, Aphrodite, sparta

In Greek religion and mythology, Ares is the Olympian god of courage, warfare, violence, physical slaughter, and the brutal aspects of battle. He stands in stark contrast to his half-sister Athena, who represents military strategy, tactical skill, and just defence. Ares embodies raw, untamed physical aggression and bloodlust.

Family and Companions:
Ares is the legitimate son of Zeus and Hera. Because of his quarrelsome and bloodthirsty temperament, he was widely disliked by the other Olympian gods, with Zeus once remarking that Ares was the most hateful god on Olympus.
Ares rode into battle accompanied by terrifying divine attendants:
- Phobos (Fear) and Deimos (Terror or Dread), his twin sons fathered with Aphrodite.
- Enyo, the goddess of discord and destruction.
- Eris, the goddess of strife.

Affair with Aphrodite:
Ares had a famous, long-standing love affair with Aphrodite, goddess of love and beauty, who was married to Hephaestus, the divine blacksmith. When Hephaestus learned of the betrayal, he forged an unbreakable, invisible bronze net and caught the two lovers in bed, displaying them to the mockery of the Olympian gods.
Despite Greek poets portraying Ares as impulsive and occasionally humiliated in combat, the warlike Spartans revered Ares as an ideal model of fierce physical valor.
