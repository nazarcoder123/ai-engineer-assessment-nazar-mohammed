Title: Gilgamesh, King of Uruk and the Quest for Immortality
Category: Mesopotamian Mythology & Legends
Keywords: Gilgamesh, Uruk, Enkidu, Humbaba, Ishtar, Bull of Heaven, Utnapishtim, immortality

The Epic of Gilgamesh, originating in ancient Mesopotamia (Sumer and Babylon), is widely considered the earliest surviving literary masterpiece. Gilgamesh was the historical and mythological king of Uruk, described as two-thirds divine and one-third human, possessing superhuman strength, majestic beauty, and boundless energy.

Bond with Enkidu:
Initially an arrogant tyrant, Gilgamesh's rule alarmed the gods, who created Enkidu, a wild man of nature, to challenge and temper him. After wrestling to a standstill in Uruk, the two became inseparable brothers. Together, they achieved legendary feats:
- Defeated Humbaba, the terrifying monstrous guardian of the Cedar Forest.
- Slew the celestial Bull of Heaven sent by goddess Ishtar after Gilgamesh scorned her romantic advances.

The Quest for Immortality:
In punishment for slaying the Bull of Heaven, the gods struck Enkidu down with a fatal illness. Shattered by grief and terrified of his own mortality, Gilgamesh embarked on a perilous quest across the Waters of Death to find Utnapishtim, the sage who had survived the Great Deluge and been granted immortality by the gods.
Utnapishtim taught Gilgamesh that mortality is the inescapable destiny of all humankind. Though Gilgamesh failed the sleep trial and lost the plant of youth to a serpent, he returned to Uruk with wisdom, realizing that a human achieves true immortality through their enduring legacy and monuments.
